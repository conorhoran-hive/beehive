# Plain-English rules

Write for an SME owner who has never borrowed for their business and may not speak English as a first language. If they'd need to ask what a word means, change it.

## Voice

- Confident, plain and factual: a trusted adviser, not a salesperson.
- Write "you" for the reader and "we" for Beehive.
- Use short sentences with one idea each. Most should be under 20 words.
- Lead with the fact, then what it means: "Money within 48 hours of approval."
- No exclamation marks, superlatives or hype ("only 11%!", "best-in-class", "seamless", "unlock", "empower", "leverage", "solutions").
- Use active verbs: "We review your business", not "Your business will be assessed".
- Spell out every abbreviation once per document, then use the short form: small and medium-sized businesses (SMEs), Dubai Financial Services Authority (DFSA), Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC), or simply "the Gulf".
- When a technical term must stay (because it's on a form the customer will see), keep it in brackets after the plain version: "Lists of money owed to you and money you owe (ageing reports)".

Product names stay as they are (Term finance, Working capital finance). Add an "In short" line or row explaining each product in one sentence.

## Never simplify without sign-off

The following are legal or regulatory. Copy them word for word from `facts.md`. If a simpler version would help, draft it, and tell the person it needs Compliance approval:
- the DFSA regulatory line
- the rate footnote
- any banks or market-practice disclaimer

## Quick test before handing over

Read each sentence and ask: would a shop owner with no finance background understand it first time? If not, rewrite it. Then scan for any word in the left column of the swap list.
